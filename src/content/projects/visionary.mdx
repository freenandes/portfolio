Zero-to-one rewards a different muscle: defining a product that doesn't exist
yet, prototyping it fast enough to be believed, and shipping enough of it to be
real. At Smartidiom, that meant turning a traditional direct-servicing company
into a digital platform — and proving it to funders and a global audience.

## The friction

- No existing product, no patterns, no certainty — just a thesis and a small
  team.
- The platform had to connect customers with certified professionals across
  several interconnected products at once.
- Validation had to be real enough to secure funding and survive a public
  showcase.

## What I did

- Designed **and coded 4 interconnected products** from scratch in a lean
  4-person team, delivering production front-end.
- Built a **component system from scratch** that enabled **~50–75% faster**
  feature development.
- Created the platform's micro-tools: calendar, finance tracking, bidding
  systems, and ISO-compliant onboarding.
- Showcased the work at **CES** and secured **European Community funding**
  through pure product validation.

## Outcome

A traditional services business became a credible digital platform — validated
on the CES floor and funded on the strength of the product itself.

## Retrospective

In 0→1 work, the prototype *is* the argument. The fastest way to align
stakeholders and unlock funding wasn't a deck — it was a working thing they
could touch. Owning both the design and the code is what made that speed
possible.
