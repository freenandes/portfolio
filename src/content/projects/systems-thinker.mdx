The hardest problems are rarely on the screen. At Percona — open-source
databases and tooling for engineers — adoption was stalling, and the easy
explanation was "the UI looks dated." It wasn't. The friction lived in the
ecosystem: inconsistent patterns across products, a hard dependency on Grafana,
and no shared language between design and a fast-moving engineering org.

## The friction

- Three separate products had drifted into three separate design languages.
- Front-end work was slow and lossy — every handoff re-litigated the same
  decisions.
- A heavy reliance on Grafana for navigation created technical debt that
  blocked the roadmap.
- There was no analytics baseline, so "is this better?" was an opinion, not a
  measurement.

## What I did

- Built the **Peak Design System**, adopted across **3 products and 10+
  engineers**, cutting handoff and front-end development time by **~50%** and
  reaching near-100% implementation accuracy through self-serve components.
- Established a **PostHog analytics framework** so product and docs decisions
  could be made on evidence.
- Kicked off the **Native Navigation** project to retire Grafana technical debt
  and unblock the roadmap.
- Shipped production code for the internal services platform, removing handoff
  friction entirely for that surface.

## Outcome

Systematic, scalable fixes — not cosmetics — contributed to **45% PMM adoption
growth**. The system became the spec, and "is this better?" became a number.

## Retrospective

The temptation was always to ship the visible fix first. The leverage was in
the boring, structural work: the token layer, the analytics baseline, the
navigation rewrite. Naming the *system* problem — and resisting the cosmetic
one — was the whole job.
