Scale changes the nature of a design problem. At TUI — one of the world's
largest travel groups — the goal wasn't a beautiful page; it was a *system* that
could generate and govern hundreds of thousands of pages across five markets,
and move real business away from expensive metasearch dependencies.

## The friction

- Destination content was bottlenecked on hand-built pages.
- Five markets meant five subtly different experiences and five maintenance
  burdens.
- Organic traffic — and the business case for owning it — depended on shipping
  at a volume manual workflows couldn't reach.

## What I did

- Designed the **PageBuilder** system that generated **100K+ destination
  pages**, supporting **4× organic traffic growth** and reducing dependence on
  metasearch engines.
- Created the **Tagus design system**, unifying experiences across **5 global
  markets** and feeding a templating framework that accelerated multi-market
  deployment.
- Designed complete e-commerce flows from inspiration through checkout,
  standardized across markets and validated through A/B testing and Baymard
  benchmarking before scaling.
- Co-founded the TUI Lisbon hub, scaling the team and its innovation practices.

## Outcome

A content engine instead of a content backlog: more pages, more organic
traffic, less metasearch dependency — all governed by one system across five
markets.

## Retrospective

The win wasn't any single page; it was making the *next* hundred thousand pages
cheap and consistent. Designing the rules, then trusting the system to produce
the output, is a different discipline than designing screens — and the one that
actually moved the business.
